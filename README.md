# FRUS Explorer Light

FRUS Explorer Light is a self-hosted web edition of [FRUS Explorer](https://github.com/joshbotts/FRUS-Explorer), the iOS, iPadOS and macOS app for researching the *Foreign Relations of the United States* series. One container serves the corpus, its full-text index, and each researcher's notes and highlights to any modern browser.

**What works today**, from the published image `ghcr.io/joshbotts/frus-explorer-light:edge`, for amd64 and arm64.

*The corpus:*

- Import of a Mac export copied into the `/data` volume, checked and served read-only. The server refuses an export from another index version. One that holds the owner's writing has it kept in the user's research data, marked as from a research database, and removed from the served copy; a read-only server refuses it.
- Search with the app's query language, with the facets, the Query Inspector, the order by relevance or date, and the timeline and concordance readings, each saying which results it read. Each result carries its date, drawn as the app draws it, and a search you replace stops on the server.
- Browse, which lists a section's own text first, as the app does: a meeting's list of those present, say, above its minutes, and the editors' annotations of every volume, its page's sixteen layers and an axis of them across the series. The reader, with its text size and Reading Width, its links, which follow a printed page into a volume the server does not hold as 1.0 does, and the cards for people and terms. All of it is usable by keyboard and free of axe violations.
- Cite, with BibTeX and RIS, and Citation Lookup, opened by Search's Find by Citation: a citation pasted, its fields typed, or a chapter's footnotes at once, each looked up by the app's own matcher, its results labelled as the app labels them.
- Related Documents, beside Cite: the documents most related to the one you read, ranked by the app's own engine by their archival provenance, the cross-references between them, their dates, where the editors placed them, and the people and subjects they share, each reason a chip, and each row's line your own summary of it where you wrote one, with the app's scopes and weights. Project Home's Suggested Next ranks the same way, on Refresh and after an import.
- The cross-reference graph, beside Related: the documents that cite the one you read and those it cites, one to three hops out, widened when it has few, as the app's graph shows them, with the citers in volumes the server does not hold counted, the archival material its footnotes cite and FRUS did not print, and a volume's documents gathered once they pass thirty; on a timeline of their dates, with the documents close in date grouped, or as a network. Its nodes, dates and timeline are the app's own code's, its network the app's layout; every node is reached by keyboard, and the reference list beside it lists every document it draws.
- Source Explorer, beside Cite: where a document came from, its source note read by the app's own rules over the archival indexes the app bundles, a lot's NARA series with NARA's facts about it, a decimal file's filing period and scans, a presidential library's collection, the rolls a document from before 1906 was likely filed in, with the other documents drawn from the same archival source in the app's three scopes, the collection the note names with its record, and the unprinted material its footnotes point at; a volume's Sources outline in Browse; and, with a NARA Catalog key, the person's own or the server's, the National Archives Catalog searched through the server, which keeps each answer for a day and counts each person's searches with its own key, as the app's section searches it. **Look Up in NARA**, in the reader's selection bar and a footnote's, reads the archival citations in a passage, as the app's sheet does, and searches the catalogue the same way. Check 8 holds every fixture source note's record, through the API, to the app's export generator's.
- People, among Browse's axes: everyone the volumes' lists of persons name, as the index reconciles them across volumes, by letter, each with their role, years and mentions, and a sheet with the app's lifespan line, a year the name authority lacks filled from the Department's register of principal officers and chiefs of mission, Find all mentions, the VIAF and Wikidata links, their posts under Career, and the subjects of the volumes that mention them, each opening the volumes that cover it.
- Analytics, from the header's menu, as the Mac's: Corpus Analytics charts a term by year, decade, volume or subseries, in documents or occurrences, with a scope and the administrations' years, and opens the documents in Search; Person Analytics ranks the people mentioned, follows up to five over time, compares two and draws the co-mention network around one; Cross-Reference Analytics ranks the most-referenced documents, with the degree distribution, the volumes' heat matrix and PageRank's landmarks; Archival Analytics ranks the archival collections the volumes drew on by era, draws a collection's co-citation network and the editors' hand-offs, and profiles your indexed volumes; Chronology lists a range by day, month or year; and About the Series shows the four dashboards of the app's Research Guide. Every series is the app's own code's, over the served index or the data the app bundles, each chart drawn by Vega-Lite with its table beside it, its CSV with the app's method block and its figure as PNG or SVG, and parity tests hold each to the kit's over the fixtures.
- The Word Cloud, from the same menu and the reader's rail: the clouds the app bundles, of the whole corpus, each subseries and each volume, under four lenses, Topics, Actions, Concepts and Sentiment, drawn in the browser by the app's own packing with the ranked list beside it, whose words chart themselves in Corpus Analytics, open a search, or hide from one cloud, a lens or every cloud; each cloud filtered by the reader's own Word Cloud settings, which Settings ▸ Word Cloud sets as the app's pane does and which follow them to the Mac in an export. A parity test holds every cloud to the bundled file's list.
- A curator's corrections of the index everyone shares, as the app makes them, each in a log an admin reads that names who made it and when: a document reclassified as an editorial note or a document, in the Research rail's Classification, and on the People screen two people merged, a record separated, and either undone. A Standalone server applies one at once, and keeps it through every indexing after; an Import server, whose export it never writes, gives it to the next export it installs.

*The research layer,* each researcher's own, kept on the server:

- Tags: the reader's Research rail tags a document, with the app's tag picker, and Settings ▸ Tags renames, merges and deletes, each by the app's rules.
- Highlights, as the app makes and draws them: a selection in the reader offers the four colours in a bar over the text, ⇧⌘H (Ctrl+Shift+H) highlights in yellow, and a tap on a highlight asks to remove it. The Research rail's Highlights section lists the document's highlights, each with its colour to change and Remove…. Check 6 holds the reader's text between offsets to the kit's in Chromium, on every fixture row. A server with no sign-in takes a change only from a page at its own address, with a token, as JSON; `FRUS_USER_DATA=off` makes it read-only.
- Notes and summaries: notes written with the app's formatting, kept as the Mac keeps them, in the Research rail and All Notes, and summaries shown with the app's words for who wrote them, beside the researcher's own; Settings ▸ Data exports the notes as the app's Markdown files.
- Search over the researcher's own writing, as the app's search window does it: Search in chooses the documents, the notes and the summaries, My Tags filters by the user's tags, counted over the results, and each result carries its tags. The facets, the Query Inspector and the readings count the same match, and each user searches their own alone.
- Saved searches with the Mac's "+N since last run", working corpora captured from a search and browsed and searched inside, volume scopes, and Checklist Mode, kept in the browser for the session.
- Projects, with the one each user works in and Project Home; the research trail of documents visited, searches run and exports, with its switch and Session Log; the method appendix as Markdown and CSV; and the Mac's synced preferences, the citation style among them.
- Collections and their editor: sections three deep, documents, note blocks in the app's rich text, excerpts frozen from the reader's selection, the five apparatus blocks, the inspector and section defaults, the app's six templates and the order by date. A live preview sits beside the contents, and the HTML, Word, BibTeX and RIS exports are each the kit's to the byte, checked for quotations first and kept in the history. A collection's `.fruscollection` file goes both ways, and one the web wrote opens whole in the Mac app; a smart collection follows a saved search, with Create Static Snapshot.
- Send to Zotero Library, from Cite and a collection's export sheet, with each person's own Zotero key: the document, or the collection's documents in a Zotero collection of its name, into their Zotero library with their tags and research notes, as the app sends them, the collection's kept in the history.
- The Research page, the app's Research tab: every document the researcher has annotated, by collection, tag and highlight colour, as the app's own code gathers them, with Contains Notes, All Notes and History.
- The Mac's JSON export imported into the server's research store, from Settings ▸ Data, by the kit's own rules, the app's Import from JSON…'s: every kind of record a file of format version 1 to 7 carries, each under its Mac id, a copy already held replaced only by a newer one, a note's formatting and a summary's author going with a text an older export replaces, with a report of what came in, was replaced or was kept. The same screen exports the researcher's data as the Mac's own file, with one key of the web's, which imports here again unchanged, and which the app imports with Settings ▸ System ▸ Data & Recovery ▸ Import from JSON… on a Mac, iPhone or iPad.

*Running it:*

- Serving at the root of a host or under a path behind a reverse proxy, such as `https://example.org/frus-explorer/`, as `FRUS_PUBLIC_URL` says, with an nginx recipe for each in the install guide.
- Local accounts, `FRUS_AUTH=local`: an admin makes each, the first with `frus-light user add`, the rest there or in Administration, each signs in on the server's own page, and passwords are hashed with Argon2id ([`docs/INSTALL.md`](docs/INSTALL.md#accounts)).
- Standalone mode, with `compose.standalone.yaml` ([`docs/INSTALL.md`](docs/INSTALL.md#standalone-mode)): the server downloads volumes from GitHub, with their figure images from history.state.gov, each file checked against GitHub's listing, and indexes them itself with the app's own indexer, a volume at a time while search answers; or a seed from a Mac export. Administration ▸ Library downloads, indexes, side-loads a TEI file of your own, and removes a volume from the index and the disk; Status compacts and checks the index, and resets it. A new release's index version re-indexes the volumes at the start, while search answers. Check for Corrections and Update bring the Office of the Historian's corrected volumes, and each researcher who annotated a changed document sees the app's banner and reviews it, with the highlights moved where the app's own search finds their words again; an Import server compares its export with its TEI folder for the same review.
- Administration, for an admin ([`docs/INSTALL.md`](docs/INSTALL.md#administration)): the server's status; its jobs, an index's import, a JSON import, an index's reset and from phase 3 the library's, run one at a time, kept across a restart, and stopped between their steps by Cancel…; the users and, with accounts, their roles, passwords and disabling, never leaving the server without an admin; the backups, with the newest to download; and the server's NARA key. Its two recovery actions ask first: erase one user's research, after naming what they hold, and reset the index in Import mode.
- Stored keys: each person's NARA Catalog and Zotero keys in Settings ▸ Keys, and the server's NARA key, kept sealed under a key file no backup holds, and never shown again ([`docs/INSTALL.md`](docs/INSTALL.md#the-key-file)). The server asks NARA and Zotero with them for the person who asks, and NARA with `FRUS_NARA_API_KEY` where no key is stored, as on a server that keeps no user data.
- A collection's PDF, through the optional Gotenberg container of Compose's `pdf` profile, which the export sheet offers only while it answers ([`docs/INSTALL.md`](docs/INSTALL.md#pdf-export)).
- Sign-in through that proxy, `FRUS_AUTH=header`: the proxy signs people in and names each in a header the server takes from the proxy's address alone, and each keeps research of their own, named at the top of every page, with a footer saying that whoever runs the instance can read it. The install guide's recipe checks a session with nginx and oauth2-proxy.
- The research store in `app.db`, the Compose install's, or in PostgreSQL, for a shared deployment that chooses one with `FRUS_USER_STORE`, every store method held alike on both by one conformance suite in CI.
- A daily backup of the research store, at 03:00 in the container's time zone or soon after a Mac that slept through it wakes, none when nothing changed, seven kept in the volume and seven more in `./backups` on the host; `frus-light restore` puts one back, and `GET /api/v1/admin/backup` gives the newest with a manifest of versions. The owner's restore drill found their research again.
- Parity with the Mac app, checked in CI on three fixture volumes: the same index rows, search results, document HTML, citation lookups and Source Explorer records. At phase 3's sign-off, on the full corpus as well: a Standalone server's index held row for row to the Mac's full export, and its Citation Lookup and Source Explorer to the app's own engine and export generator ([`docs/SPEC.md`](docs/SPEC.md#verification), checks 2, 7 and 8). The server compiles the app's own Swift code from a pinned submodule.
- A daily CI watch of the app's `v2` branch, which reports in one issue what the next pin move has to handle.
- Releases, cut by hand from a build that passed its smoke test and named for the FRUS Explorer build whose exports they serve, such as `1.0-1861.1`, the first, published on 22 November. Their notes name the image's digest and the index version, saying when it changed, and [`docs/INSTALL.md`](docs/INSTALL.md#releases) says how to follow them.

## Development plan

The edition is built in phases 0 to 6, the last optional, each a visible slice with its own exit test from the verification table in [`docs/SPEC.md`](docs/SPEC.md#verification). Within a phase, work comes in sessions of one pull request each, on two tracks. On the core track, code the server needs from the app moves into the app's shared `FRUSCoreKit` by pull requests on [`FRUS-Explorer`](https://github.com/joshbotts/FRUS-Explorer), which the owner checks on a Mac and merges, and a pin move brings each into this build. The server and browser-app track runs without the Mac. [`docs/PLAN.md`](docs/PLAN.md) lists every session with its done-when, and the owner's checkpoints.

## Goals

- **Keep the research tools.** Browse, full-text search with the app's query language, the reader with highlights and notes, collections and their exports, citation tools, Source Explorer and analytics all stay. Search by meaning follows in a later phase.
- **Match the Mac app's results.** On the same inputs, the web edition must produce the same index rows, the same search results in the same order, and the same document HTML.
- **Serve a researcher or a small group.** One person on a laptop or a home server, or a class or team sharing one instance. It is not designed as a public, high-traffic site.
- **Deploy simply.** Docker Compose on a Mac or a Linux host first; a managed deployment on AWS follows in phase 6.

"Light" leaves out what depends on Apple-only frameworks: summary generation, iCloud sync, widgets and Live Activities, Spotlight, Handoff, TipKit tips, Audio Graphs and native multi-window scenes. Browser tabs replace windows, and data tables replace Audio Graphs. Summaries imported from the app are shown with their authorship labels, and people can write their own.

## Architecture

A Swift 6 server owns two SQLite files: `frus.db`, the corpus index, and `app.db`, the users and their data, which a shared deployment may keep in PostgreSQL instead. The browser runs a TypeScript single-page app and shows document HTML that the server renders.

```mermaid

  B[Browser] -->|localhost:8080| S
  subgraph C[Docker Compose on a Mac or a Linux host]
    S[frus-light server<br/>Swift 6, Hummingbird] --- I[(frus.db<br/>imported Mac export)]
    S --- U[(app.db<br/>users and their data)]
    S -.-> P[Gotenberg<br/>optional PDF]
  end
  T[/TEI volumes folder<br/>mounted read-only/] --> S
  S -.-> X[GitHub, NARA, Zotero]
```

The diagram shows the Docker Compose install that ships first, with both databases in the `/data` volume. From phase 6, the same image also runs on AWS as one ECS task, with the index copied from S3.

| Part | Technology | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Server | Swift 6 on Linux, Hummingbird 2 | REST API, sessions, jobs, and the single-page app's static files |
| Shared core | The Mac app's own Swift files, compiled from a pinned `FRUS-Explorer` submodule | Indexing, search and its query language, TEI parsing and HTML, citations, Source Explorer |
| Corpus index | SQLite FTS5 with the `porter unicode61` tokenizer | The Mac index's schema, at the pinned index version |
| User store | SQLite, with FTS5 over each user's notes and summaries | Notes, highlights, tags, collections, projects, users and sessions |
| Web client | TypeScript, React and Vite | Every screen. The reader reuses the app's own HTML, CSS and JavaScript in a sandboxed iframe |
| Optional services | `llama-server` running EmbeddingGemma; Gotenberg | Search by meaning; PDF export |

### Design decisions

| Decision | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| The index lives on the server, and the browser is a thin client | A container has real disk and CPU, so SQLite FTS5 runs natively, and one copy of user data needs no sync engine |
| The server compiles the Mac app's own Swift files | Query parsing, source-note parsing, citation matching and TEI rendering are large and heavily tested, and a second implementation would drift |
| The index comes from a Mac export, or the server builds it from TEI | The Mac's Export Research Database… file is version-stamped and verifiable, so serving it ships first; indexing on Linux follows |
| User data lives apart from the corpus index | The Mac index copies notes, summaries and tags into corpus rows, which a shared server cannot do |
| Summaries are shown and hand-written, never generated | Apple's on-device FoundationModels framework has no Linux equivalent |
| The reader reuses the app's rendering assets | Highlight offsets depend on that exact document structure |

## Deployment

- **Docker Compose, first:** one image and one Compose file on a Mac or a Linux host, listening on 127.0.0.1. Everything the server keeps lives in a named volume at `/data`, because SQLite's write-ahead log needs a local filesystem, and on a Mac a folder shared from macOS into Docker's virtual machine is not one. A folder of TEI volumes is mounted read-only, and a Mac export is imported with `docker compose cp`. CI publishes the image to GitHub Container Registry for amd64 and arm64.
- **AWS, in phase 6:** one ECS task on Fargate, in private subnets behind an Application Load Balancer that handles sign-in. At start the task copies a read-only snapshot of the index from S3 and opens it with SQLite's `immutable=1`. User data stays in SQLite, replicated to S3 by Litestream. A deploy stops the old task before starting the new one, trading a short outage for a single writer and no database service. The estimate is about $155–170 a month.
